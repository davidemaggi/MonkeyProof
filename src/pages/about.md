## Welcome to My Digital Corner

I'm Davide, System Architect and Manager at Accenture Industry X. I design the architecture of a quality management platform and lead the team that builds it, which means I spend my days drawing boxes and arrows, then making sure the code agrees with them. I've been writing software since 2006, so roughly twenty years of turning coffee into distributed systems. These days I live mostly in the backend (.NET, messaging, Kubernetes), designing systems that are meant to survive both scale and developers.

This space is where curiosity becomes experiments, experiments become articles, and articles occasionally become things I later refactor.

---

## The Long Way Round

I didn't start as an architect. Right after high school I went freelance and built mobile and web apps, poking at every technology that looked fun along the way: .NET, Java, Android, iOS, Python, PHP. Eight years of that teaches you a lot, mostly by breaking things in front of customers.

In 2015 I joined SpecTec as a software tester. Yes, I broke things *professionally*. I worked my way up to leading test automation and then ran the QA department. I recommend it to every future architect: you see every shortcut a design took, from the receiving end.

Then came Engineering. I started as a business analyst at Carnival because of my background in asset management, and promptly steered myself back into a technical role by proposing and building a corporation-wide asset management solution. After that I worked as a solution architect for cruise and travel companies like Carnival, MSC, GNV, and Alpitour, led development at Alpitour, and ended up as Head of Java developments for the Travel, Hospitality & Logistics business unit. Yes, the .NET guy ran a Java practice. *Things happen.*

In 2022 I moved to Accenture, back to .NET, microservices, and a stack long enough to need its own legend.

Twenty years in, I've learned that complexity is easy, simplicity is hard, and every "quick solution" eventually becomes a meeting.

## What I Do

### Backend & Architecture

Designing distributed systems, defining boundaries, and trying to keep microservices from multiplying after midnight. Most of my work revolves around .NET, APIs, messaging, and making systems behave in production, where diagrams go to meet reality.

### Managing (Yes, Really)

Somewhere along the way the job became half people. Next to the architecture I manage the team: I mentor developers, keep goals and deliverables aligned with the business, and sit in the meetings that decide what actually gets built. I still get my hands dirty in the code when nobody's looking. *Managers who can't read a stack trace make me nervous, so I try not to become one.*

### Continuous Learning

Technology moves fast. Architects either keep learning or slowly become legacy systems themselves, and I'd rather not be deprecated before version 2.0 of me ships.

I live in Rapallo, on the Ligurian coast, which is a dangerous place for someone who should be writing articles instead of staring at the sea.

Before I was refereeing pull requests, I played water polo in Serie A, Italy's top league, and then refereed there too. It turns out both jobs involve a lot of people insisting they did nothing wrong, except in the pool they're also trying to drown each other.

## This Blog

MonkeyProof is a place for *chaos, but technically accurate chaos*.

I write about software architecture, backend development, distributed systems, and whatever technical rabbit hole I've fallen into this month. Some posts are long explorations, some are experiments, and some are lessons learned the hard way so you don't have to.

Think of it as notes from someone who's been in the industry long enough to know that:

- There is no silver bullet
- Every system becomes legacy eventually
- The simplest solution is usually discovered right after shipping the complex one
